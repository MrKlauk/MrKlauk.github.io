Flatground Beginner

Ollie

← All Tricks

The ollie is the single most important trick in skateboarding. Almost every other trick is built on the mechanics of the ollie. Invented on vert by Alan 'Ollie' Gelfand in 1978 and on flatground by Rodney Mullen in 1982, it works through a combination of tail pop and front foot drag that levels the board in the air without your hands. Once your ollie is consistent and clean, the rest of skateboarding opens up.

Prerequisites

Basic riding and pushing Comfortable stopping Confident balance on the board

Common Mistakes

  • Slamming the tail in the ground, it's more like dribbling a basketball, not stomping the tail.
  • Jumping too early before the tail hits the ground.
  • Front foot not dragging high enough, resulting in a rocketed ollie.
  • Landing with feet too close together, aim for shoulder-width.

How To Do It

  1. Place the ball of your foot in the middle of the tail, with your weight mostly on the front foot. Front foot sits behind the front bolts.
  2. Bend your knees and jump, snapping the tail down hard against the ground at the end of it.
  3. As the tail hits, drag your front foot up toward the nose, leveling the board.
  4. Suck your knees up to your chest like Spider-Man to give the board room to rise beneath you.
  5. Land with both feet over the bolts and absorb the impact by bending your knees.
